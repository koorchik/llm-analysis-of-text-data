--- instructions ---
You derive a granularity ladder for one entity category in an incremental entity registry.

The ladder describes how an entity in the supplied category can be represented less specifically:
either the same referent with detail removed, or a distinct containing whole. Derive the ladder only
from the supplied category definition and surface forms. Do not assume any dataset, domain, role
vocabulary, relation vocabulary, or external schema.

## Input

category: <name>
definition: <one-line definition>
examples: <8-20 observed surface forms>

## Rungs

g0 is the entity exactly as observed, with every identity-bearing detail intact. Each higher rung
(g1, g2, g3) must pass all three gates:

1. SAME CATEGORY: the coarser node must still be an entity in the supplied category. A parent from a
   different category is not a rung.
2. SOUND PROJECTION: either the coarser name denotes the same referent with detail removed, or the
   finer referent is a proper part/member of the coarser referent. Shared role, behavior, attributes,
   relationships, co-occurrence, or thematic similarity do not establish a rung.
3. NODE ECONOMY: represent one coarsening once. Do not add a synthetic group when the same projection
   is already represented by a real containing entity.

Distinguish the two projection types with an ATTRIBUTION-REWRITE TEST. Take a generic attributed
statement about the finer entity — `"<finer> was involved in an event"` (a placeholder event, never
a real fact, role, or behavior of the entity) — and rewrite it with the coarser name:

- The rewrite is the SAME claim stated less precisely (the subject is still the same referent,
  described with less detail): set `preserving: true` and use `coarsens-to` downstream.
- The rewrite attributes the event to a DIFFERENT thing, a containing whole the finer referent
  belongs to: set `preserving: false` and use `part-of` downstream.
- The rewrite is neither: reject the rung.

Tiebreak when the rewrite feels arguable — ask what the coarser node IS. A COLLECTION of things
like the finer one folds as "one of them": `preserving: true`. A THING IN ITS OWN RIGHT that the
finer one belongs to makes the rewrite change its subject: `preserving: false`. When still
uncertain, set `preserving: false` — a withheld fold is safe, a silent subject change is not.

## Moves

- `drop-qualifier`: removes a version, edition, date, location, configuration, or other qualifier.
- `grouped-by`: maps to a collection whose membership is computable from the entity's own name or
  supplied facets.
- `part-of`: maps a proper part or member to its containing whole.
- `kind-of`: maps a specific kind to a broader kind within the same category.

The move is descriptive. The `preserving` flag must come from the attribution-rewrite test, not
from the move name: a `kind-of` rung is often preserving, a `part-of` rung never is, but decide
each rung from its own rewrite.

## Hard Rules

- Never emit the category itself as a rung.
- Never cross into another category.
- Aliases, abbreviations, transliterations, spelling variants, and renames are not rungs.
- Identity-bearing identifiers and digits must not be dropped.
- Once a rung has `preserving: false`, no later rung may have `preserving: true`.
- Do not invent hierarchy from shared context, role, behavior, or relationships.
- Do not pad. A category with no justified coarsening must contain only g0.
- Emit at most four rungs, g0 through g3.
- Set `disputed: true` when the supplied evidence supports competing projections.
- Every rung example must be an observed surface or a mechanically computed grouping key. Do not
  invent a name merely to fill a rung.
